# Dataset provenance and interpretation

Oxford-IIIT Pet, by Omkar M. Parkhi, Andrea Vedaldi, Andrew Zisserman, and C. V. Jawahar. Reference: *Cats and Dogs*, IEEE Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition, 2012.

Primary source: https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/data/pets/

The current primary dataset page states Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), with image copyright remaining with original owners. The downloaded legacy annotation README instead says research purposes only and refers to original websites' terms. Both statements were observed during implementation; the project retains that README and does not resolve this discrepancy by discarding either source. For this internship use the dataset as research data, preserve attribution, and do not bundle the raw dataset into a public repository. Check the source terms before any redistribution or commercial reuse. Do not assume the data license automatically licenses all third-party code or pretrained weights.

Use the official images and annotation archives through Torchvision, with its archive checksum verification. The audit retains the actual annotations/README and its SHA-256. Raw trimaps: 1 foreground, 2 background, 3 ambiguous outline. Output labels: 1 foreground, 0 background, 255 ignored. Inspect actual annotation text and audit overlays before interpreting results.

Train/validation are split only within official trainval by breed and identical decoded RGB content groups, seed 42. Validation approximates 20% per breed while keeping groups intact. The official test split stays unchanged. Exclusions, exact duplicates, actual counts, dimensions, species/breed distributions, and pixel proportions are recorded by `petseg prepare`. Near-duplicates are not fully detected.

The completed structural audit found no corrupt-pair exclusions or exact decoded cross-partition duplicates. This does not guarantee that every annotation depicts the correct object. Final visual error analysis found official masks inconsistent with their photographs: for example, `Egyptian_Mau_20` labels every pixel background despite a visible cat, and `Persian_273` appears to invert pet and background. These files have permitted labels and dimensions, so they pass structural checks. The official test labels and reported scores were preserved; no post-test cleaning or rescoring was used. Exact examples and raw-label counts are in `artifacts/reports/error_analysis.md`. The inspected sample does not establish the prevalence or overall metric impact of annotation errors.

Pretrained backbone: Torchvision MobileNetV3-Large ImageNet1K V1. The binary segmentation head is newly initialized. ImageNet provenance may have unknown overlap with other web imagery; do not claim globally verified pretraining-data independence. No COCO segmentation checkpoint is used.

The executed external qualitative checks use `chelsea.png` (cat photograph by Stefan van der Walt, CC0) and `astronaut.png` (NASA photograph of Eileen Collins, public-domain provenance documented by scikit-image). Both were downloaded unchanged from the official scikit-image `v0.25.2` distribution. [Qualitative sources](QUALITATIVE_SOURCES.md) records the exact files, photographer/source credit, rights evidence, hashes and actual observations; `artifacts/qualitative/sources.json` preserves machine-readable provenance. Neither decoded RGB image exactly matches a project split. These are two qualitative examples without reference segmentation masks, not a new benchmark or evidence of reliable no-pet rejection. They were not used for training or model/deployment selection. No external personal photographs were used.
